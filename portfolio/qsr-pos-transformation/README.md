# Global QSR POS Transformation

> **Sanitized case study:** This overview is based on Roger Barahona’s documented professional experience. It intentionally excludes confidential, proprietary, and employer-sensitive information.

## Overview

As a **Senior Product Owner at Subway (2023–2025)**, I worked across a global POS and omnichannel restaurant technology ecosystem supporting **30,000+ restaurants**. The product environment connected in-store POS, mobile ordering, third-party delivery, and loyalty while serving markets with different operational and business needs.

My role focused on translating complex stakeholder needs into clear product direction and engineering-ready work through **roadmaps, requirements, backlog prioritization, user stories, and acceptance criteria**.

## The Challenge

A global restaurant technology platform has to balance enterprise consistency with the realities of restaurant operations and regional market needs.

The challenge was not simply delivering individual features. Product decisions had to account for a connected ecosystem spanning in-store POS, mobile ordering, third-party delivery, loyalty, restaurant operations, and regional requirements.

## My Role

As Senior Product Owner, my documented responsibilities included:

- Defining and supporting product roadmaps
- Translating stakeholder needs into product and business requirements
- Prioritizing backlogs
- Creating user stories and acceptance criteria
- Aligning cross-functional stakeholders around delivery
- Supporting release execution across a large-scale restaurant technology environment
- Working within a global product context spanning North America, LATAM, and other markets

## Product Approach

### 1. Understand the operational problem
Start with the business and restaurant need rather than jumping directly to a feature. Clarify the problem, affected users, operational impact, dependencies, and expected outcome.

### 2. Translate needs into product requirements
Convert stakeholder input into clear requirements, user stories, and acceptance criteria so engineering and delivery teams understand both **what** is needed and **why**.

### 3. Prioritize within the broader ecosystem
Evaluate work in the context of the roadmap and connected channels. POS decisions may interact with mobile ordering, delivery, loyalty, restaurant operations, or market-specific needs.

### 4. Align stakeholders and delivery teams
Create shared understanding across product, business, technology, and operational stakeholders before and during delivery.

### 5. Support release readiness
Maintain clarity through implementation and release activities so the delivered capability remains aligned with the original business need.

## What This Case Study Demonstrates

This work demonstrates my approach to product leadership in complex operational environments:

**Understand the problem → align stakeholders → define clear requirements → prioritize the work → enable delivery → support release readiness.**

The core lesson is that large-scale product ownership is not only about managing a backlog. It is about creating enough clarity and alignment for multiple teams and stakeholders to move a connected product ecosystem forward without losing sight of restaurant operations.
