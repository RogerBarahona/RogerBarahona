# Omnichannel Delivery Integration

> **Sanitized case study:** This overview is based on Roger Barahona’s documented professional experience. It intentionally excludes confidential, proprietary, and employer-sensitive information.

## Overview

During my Subway tenure, I worked on restaurant technology initiatives connecting **third-party delivery channels with the POS ecosystem**, including integrations with **Uber Eats and DoorDash**. My broader work also included curbside ordering and an omnichannel environment spanning in-store POS, mobile ordering, delivery, and loyalty.

The product challenge was to extend ordering beyond owned restaurant channels while keeping the restaurant technology ecosystem operationally coherent.

## The Challenge

Third-party delivery is not an isolated customer-facing feature. Orders ultimately intersect with restaurant systems and workflows.

The work therefore required attention to the relationship between external ordering channels and the existing POS environment, while coordinating business and technology stakeholders around requirements, testing, delivery, and restaurant operations.

## My Role

My documented experience included:

- Supporting Uber Eats and DoorDash integrations with the Subway POS ecosystem
- Translating business and operational needs into requirements
- Coordinating across stakeholders and technology teams
- Supporting testing and delivery activities
- Working within a broader omnichannel environment spanning POS, mobile ordering, delivery, and loyalty
- Supporting curbside ordering as restaurant ordering needs evolved

## Product Approach

### 1. Start with the end-to-end restaurant journey
Treat delivery as part of the restaurant operating ecosystem rather than as a standalone digital channel.

### 2. Clarify integration requirements
Translate business needs into clear requirements and expected system behavior so teams have a shared understanding of the integration.

### 3. Account for operational dependencies
Consider how ordering-channel changes interact with POS workflows, restaurant operations, testing, releases, and connected capabilities.

### 4. Align cross-functional stakeholders
Keep business, product, technology, and operational stakeholders aligned as requirements move toward implementation.

### 5. Support validation and release
Use testing and delivery discipline to help ensure the implemented capability reflects the intended requirements before release.

## What This Case Study Demonstrates

The work illustrates an important principle in omnichannel product delivery: **the customer channel and the operational platform cannot be treated separately**.

Successful integration work requires understanding the business need, defining clear system requirements, accounting for dependencies, and maintaining alignment across the teams responsible for delivering and operating the experience.
